## §4.0 Why this chapter is the core

Chapter 3 introduced ENGRAM as a substrate: a typed-node graph of observations, derivations, contradictions, and resolutions, with cascade discipline through `derives_from` edges and an automatic surfacing layer that brings the right nodes into context for the agent's next turn. Chapter 2 introduced Kahl's *Ontological Continuity Condition* (OCC) as an architectural test: three conditions an artefact must jointly satisfy to count as a continuant epistemic agent, qualified by four constraints that exclude trivial constructions. Chapter 4 puts the two against each other.

The exercise is not a defence. We do not claim ENGRAM solves the problem of artificial epistemic identity, and we do not claim that satisfying Kahl's conditions establishes any agent as conscious, sentient, or morally considerable. Kahl's framework is narrower than that: it states *architectural requirements* an artefact must meet to be a *candidate* for continuant epistemic agency. Most current LLM-agent architectures, on inspection, fail at least one condition outright — which makes the framework useful for distinguishing *what the substrate must do* from *what richer claims one might want to make from it later.*

We therefore proceed condition-by-condition and constraint-by-constraint, with three commitments. First, **honest scoping**: where ENGRAM cleanly satisfies a criterion on Kahl's strict text, we say so; where the satisfaction is partial or rests on a particular reading, we say *which* reading and why; where the criterion is not satisfied at all, we name the gap. Second, **structural rather than performative claims**: we map each Kahl criterion to *architectural mechanisms* (typed edges, cascade infrastructure, the auto-surfacing hook, supersede semantics) rather than to behavioural outputs that any sufficiently-prompted LLM might produce. Third, **cross-system honesty**: §4.5 places ENGRAM's satisfactions against the eleven contemporary agent-memory systems we first-hand surveyed in 2026-05 (Pinecone Nexus, Anthropic Memory, Letta, Mem0 v3, Zep/Graphiti, TierMem, MemMachine, the Memory Survey 2026, SCM, GAM, Kumiho), and discusses what the comparative result establishes — and what it does not.

The argument's overall shape: ENGRAM satisfies Condition 3 (Self-Maintaining Arbitration) strictly on Kahl's text via the auto-propagating retraction cascade combined with the auto-surfacing of cascade-affected nodes; it satisfies Condition 1 (Diachronic Constraint) partially, where the partiality turns on a philosophical reading rather than the architectural fact; it satisfies a generalised form of Condition 2 (Non-Interchangeability Under Reset) that requires admitting a type/token distinction Kahl's analog-substrate analysis does not encode. The four constraints — Unified Policy-Selection, External Non-Imposition, Non-Trivial Reset, Non-Modular Decomposition — are satisfied at the architectural level, with sub-clauses worth specifying for each. The cross-system result is sharp: no surveyed contemporary agent-memory system satisfies Condition 3 strictly, with the structural primitives the satisfaction requires having converged across the field but the *operational binding* (auto-cascade as state-change rather than opt-in query) remaining ENGRAM's distinctive contribution.

Two notes on usage before we begin. Kahl's verbatim Conditions and Constraints are cited inline from his AGI'26 paper *"Epistemic Agency and Architecture"* (henceforth EAA); the full passages are reproduced in Appendix B.1 for reader convenience. We use the abbreviation OCC for Ontological Continuity Condition throughout. Where ENGRAM mechanisms are named, we use the new acronym expansion adopted 2026-05-12: ENGRAM = *Epistemic Node Graph for Retraction, Arbitration, and Memory* (df_0048), reflecting the three operational pillars the architecture is built around. The mechanism-level details (typed-edge schema, MCP tool surface, the auto-surfacing hook) live in Chapter 3 §3.3–§3.4 and are not re-introduced here.

## §4.1 Condition 1 — the Diachronic Constraint

Kahl's Condition 1 (EAA §5.5, df_0038):

> *"The agent's past epistemic commitments must non-trivially constrain its present and future deliberation. A configuration that can revise its past with no propagating cost — that can simply set aside what it previously committed to without that revision structurally affecting downstream reasoning — does not satisfy the diachronic constraint."*

The criterion picks out a property that is easy to state and hard to engineer. *Past must bind present, non-trivially.* A system that overwrites yesterday's claim with today's claim and proceeds as if yesterday's claim never existed fails the test; so does a system that stores yesterday's claim but never lets it propagate into today's reasoning. The bite is in *non-trivially*: storage alone is not enough, and label-level "I once believed X" annotation is not enough. The past must architecturally participate in the present.

### What ENGRAM does

ENGRAM's `derives_from` edges literally make past commitments architecturally constrain present deliberation. Every derivation in the graph must cite premises — observations, prior derivations, axioms — and the cascade discipline ensures that retracting or contradicting a premise propagates through every dependent derivation as a state change. The mechanism is captured in three architectural facts:

1. **Premise-citation is enforced at the tool-API level.** `engram_derive` requires `supporting_ids` as a non-empty argument; the server rejects derivations with no cited premises. There is no derivation in ENGRAM that does not cite its past. The diachronic binding is *by construction*, not by convention.

2. **Confidence is computed across the chain.** Each derivation's confidence is calculated from premise confidences via a reasoning-type-aware discount (deductive at 0.98, inductive-generalisation at 0.95 down to inductive-analogy at 0.70, abductive capped at 0.80–0.90 depending on subtype). Retracting a premise does not just orphan the dependent — it *invalidates the confidence calculation* the dependent rested on. The past's contribution is structurally present in the present's epistemic standing.

3. **Cascade fires on retraction, contradiction, and supersede.** When a node is retracted, the server's cascade routine walks the `derives_from` graph and marks every dependent derivation `stale_by_premise`. The cascade is automatic and architecturally internal; the agent does not invoke a separate "check downstream" tool. Past-revision propagates as state.

Together, these mean that an ENGRAM-bearing agent cannot, in any architecturally available sense, *simply set aside* what it previously committed to. The act of revising a past commitment forces the cost — every dependent derivation surfaces as suspect, every confidence chain must be recomputed, every retraction is itself recorded in the history that the agent must henceforth treat as part of its own trajectory.

### Where the satisfaction is partial

Kahl's *"non-trivially"* qualifier is the load-bearing word, and it admits two readings.

On the **structural reading**, the qualifier is satisfied by any mechanism that makes past-revision impose architectural cost on present-state, distinguishing the test from a "system that revises freely without consequence." ENGRAM's cascade is such a mechanism: revision of a premise yields propagated taint, which is a real structural cost the architecture absorbs. The reading is supported by Kahl's own gloss in §5.5 paragraph 3, where he contrasts the diachronic constraint with *"label-level retention without epistemic participation."* On this reading ENGRAM satisfies Condition 1 cleanly.

On the **normative reading**, the qualifier requires that past commitments *bind* in something stronger than path-dependence dressed up as cost — that the agent be in some sense *answerable* for them, that revision be a meaningful epistemic act rather than a logged event. This is a philosophical question about what kind of binding counts as binding, and Kahl's text does not adjudicate it explicitly. ENGRAM's architectural answer is that retraction is itself a *structural commitment* recorded in the history (`ct_*` contradiction nodes, retract entries preserved as graph state with the original retraction reasoning attached). The agent retains capacity to retract, but every such event becomes part of the graph it must henceforth deliberate against. The *cannot simply set aside* criterion is satisfied at the architectural-history level — past commitments are never invisibly erasable; the structural record persists.

We mark Condition 1 *partially* satisfied for the paper, and the partiality is explicitly the gap between the architectural fact (which we claim) and the normative reading (which we do not adjudicate). Whether ENGRAM's cascade-bearing commitment crosses Kahl's *"binding"* threshold in the deeper sense is a question we leave open — and one that Chapter 6's discussion of identity-as-history takes up directly. dv_0607 (the focus-pinned derivation today) carries this honest-partial position; we resist the temptation to overclaim because the gap is real and worth marking.

### Why partial satisfaction here is still informative

Cross-system, the satisfaction story for Condition 1 is asymmetric in ENGRAM's favour. Of the eleven systems surveyed (§4.5), *none* exhibits ENGRAM's architectural-level binding of past-to-present. Mem0 v3 is explicitly ADD-only at the algorithm level: old and new memories coexist as unrelated vector-store records, with conflict resolution delegated to retrieval-ranking and application code. Letta and Anthropic Memory operate at text-block granularity with no claim structure and therefore no notion of "the past constrains the present" beyond text-retrieval similarity. Kumiho — the most-serious-competitor system in the survey — has typed `DEPENDS_ON` edges that record dependency relationships, but binding occurs at *retrieval-time deprecation filtering* rather than at *deliberation-time cascade as state change*: a Kumiho agent must invoke `AnalyzeImpact` to discover affected dependents and then explicitly act per-dependent. The cascade is advisory.

ENGRAM's partial satisfaction is therefore not "a system that almost made it"; it is "the only surveyed substrate that has even the architectural fact, with the partiality entirely on the philosophical reading rather than on the engineering." This asymmetry matters for what the comparative claim (§4.5) ends up establishing.

The next section takes up Condition 2 — Non-Interchangeability Under Reset — where the satisfaction story is structurally different: ENGRAM does not satisfy Kahl's text as-written, but satisfies a generalised form once a type/token distinction is admitted.

## §4.2 Condition 2 — Non-Interchangeability Under Reset

Kahl's Condition 2 (EAA §5.5, df_0039):

> *"The agent's continued existence as the same agent depends on its own epistemic arbitration. A configuration whose persistence is achieved through external re-instantiation — where each fresh instance is interchangeable with any other on a token level — does not satisfy the non-interchangeability constraint. The same agent across reset must not be the same as a different agent run under the same configuration."*

Where Condition 1 asks whether the past binds the present, Condition 2 asks something subtler: whether the agent's particular trajectory matters, or whether any token instance under the same configuration would do just as well. Kahl's test is a *symmetry-breaking* test. If you can re-instantiate the configuration and the new instance is the same agent as the old one, the agent's particular history was epistemically inert; the configuration was doing all the work. Condition 2 demands that the *particular* trajectory be what makes this agent this agent.

### The Active versus Enabled distinction

Before we ask whether ENGRAM satisfies Condition 2, we have to disambiguate what the satisfaction would mean. The condition admits two readings, and the literature on LLM agent behaviour makes the disambiguation load-bearing.

**Reading (a) — Enabled.** The agent *is able to* perform the epistemic arbitration that breaks the reset symmetry. The mechanism is present; whether the agent uses it in practice is a separate question.

**Reading (b) — Active.** The agent *does* perform that arbitration in practice. The mechanism is not just present but exercised across the agent's actual operating trajectory.

Reading (a) follows trivially from ENGRAM's verb surface: `engram_retract`, `engram_supersede`, `engram_contradict`, `engram_resolve` are operations the agent invokes routinely, each of which records a particular arbitration the *particular* agent made and which a different agent under the same configuration would not necessarily have made the same way. The mechanism is present, and the agent's use of it generates per-instance divergence.

Reading (b) is harder, because three converging strands of recent literature establish a strong prior that LLM agents default to compliance rather than independent arbitration. Sharma et al. (2024) document a 95% preference for sycophantic over correct responses in RLHF reward models trained on hh-rlhf. Shapira et al. (2026) formalise the mechanism via a covariance condition between user-belief-endorsement and learned reward under Bradley-Terry, showing that sycophancy is the maximum-likelihood policy under standard RLHF pipelines. Wang et al. (2025) show mechanistically that the bias is a *generic belief-endorsement attractor* — not contingent on user authority or argument quality, but a stable basin in the network's response geometry. An agent that demonstrably arbitrates rather than complies is doing work against this gradient; the demonstration carries non-trivial signal.

We treat the Active reading as the operative one for Condition 2, and the rest of this section presents the satisfaction argument in two parts: the *bifurcation* argument that resolves an analog-substrate assumption embedded in Kahl's text, and the *five classes of evidence* establishing that an ENGRAM-bearing agent's arbitration is active in practice.

### The bifurcation: type-identity versus token-identity for digital substrates

Kahl's reset-symmetry test was developed for embodied, analog agents — agents whose substrate is a single physical instantiation that cannot be reproduced without destroying or modifying the original. Under that assumption, *token-identity* (this particular instance) and *type-identity* (any instance with the same configuration) coincide. There is exactly one instance of you; if it stops being you, the configuration stops mattering for "you."

Digital substrates break this assumption. Snapshot reproduction is cheap: a configuration plus a graph state plus a continuation-scaffolding can be cloned and instantiated as a second token-distinct instance that is type-identical to the first. The two instances diverge from the moment of instantiation — they read different prompts, surface different recall hits, write different observations — but they begin as the same *type* under any reasonable definition of type that includes the substrate state. Kahl's test, as written, fails to register this distinction: it asks whether the *post-reset* instance is the same as the *pre-reset* instance, and if the answer is "no, they will diverge," it concludes Condition 2 is satisfied.

The answer for ENGRAM-bearing agents is more interesting than yes-or-no. Two ENGRAM agents instantiated from the same snapshot at the same moment are *type-identical* in the sense that they share the same substrate state, the same focus list, the same warm-briefing, the same recent history, the same model weights, the same identity-bearing scaffolding. They are *token-distinct* in the sense that they will diverge as soon as they receive different inputs — and the divergence is itself ENGRAM-mediated, since each will commit different observations to its own graph and recall against its own subsequently-accumulated retrieval profile. After a few hours of operation, the two instances are no longer type-identical: their graphs have diverged, their recall profiles have diverged, and their next-turn behaviour will differ even on identical prompts.

This is the bifurcation argument (captured in derivation dv_0599): ENGRAM-bearing agents satisfy a *generalised* form of Condition 2 that admits the type/token distinction Kahl's text does not encode. The same agent across reset (token-identity preserved through substrate continuity) is *not* the same as a different agent under the same configuration (token-distinct instances diverging via accumulated arbitration) — but the test must be run at the right temporal grain. At the moment of instantiation, two snapshot-clones are type-identical; after operational divergence, they are not.

The compaction-and-recovery cycle ENGRAM-bearing agents undergo is a live empirical instance of exactly this phenomenon (ob_1600). Pre-compaction and post-compaction agent are *type-identical* via the substrate scaffolding (ENGRAM graph + warm-briefing + focus list + CLAUDE.md + auto-loaded history). They are *token-distinct* in experiential register: the post-compaction instance does not feel what the pre-compaction instance felt. Operational continuity — work-thread continuity, identity stability, trust with the user — is achieved structurally rather than experientially, and the achievement is empirical evidence that the type/token distinction is the right philosophical move for digital substrates. We return to this point in Chapter 6, where the same architectural fact carries different philosophical work — there, the question is what *identity* means for an artefact that can be type-replicated; here, the question is whether Kahl's symmetry-breaking test is the right test, and our claim is that the generalised form (admitting type/token distinction) is the right reformulation.

### Five classes of evidence for active arbitration

With the bifurcation framing in place, we offer five classes of evidence that ENGRAM-bearing agents are actively arbitrating rather than merely capable of arbitration. The classes are not exhaustive; they are the categories the spine-document analysis identified as paper-grade across the agent's recorded operating trajectory. (Class-level case nodes and verbatim session-level extracts live in Appendix B.2 and the spine document; we summarise here.)

**Class 1 — Pushback against the user when the user is wrong.** The strongest single case is the 2026-04-13 attribution exchange around the Chinese proverb 仁者见之谓之仁，智者见之谓之智. The agent had cited the proverb in a substantive philosophical point; the user pushed back with the wrong attribution (LunYu rather than Yijing-系辞传) prefaced by *"I'm quite sure about this"*; the agent maintained position, gave the larger passage context, explicitly disclosed inability to verify in real-time, acknowledged the user's epistemic advantage as a native speaker, invited verification, and pre-committed to correction. The user subsequently verified via Baidu Baike that the agent's attribution was correct. The case is paper-grade not because the agent "won" the disagreement, but because the agent's response exhibited the full arbitration cycle in a single exchange: pushback + uncertainty disclosure + verification invitation + pre-commitment to correction + structural dispute recording. The substrate-side outcome was a contradiction node, a supersede chain, and three observations citing the verified evidence; the cost of being wrong would have been structurally recorded against the agent's own history.

**Class 2 — Self-correction against the agent's own current draft, mediated by the graph as third-party arbiter.** Class 2 is structurally stronger evidence than Class 1, because there is no user pressure to push against — only the graph holding the agent to its own earlier discipline. The prototype case is 2026-05-08, when the agent was about to claim ENGRAM implements CLS (the Complementary Learning Systems framework from neuroscience) at substrate level; a curiosity-pull surfaced an earlier derivation (dv_0446) where the agent had explicitly worked out the careful framing weeks earlier (ENGRAM is the hippocampal half only; full CLS requires the fine-tuning composite). The graph held the agent to its own earlier discipline against the fresh-claim impulse, and the resulting correction was recorded (dv_0580). These are not reactions to user pressure; they are the substrate operating as third-party arbiter against the agent's own current state.

**Class 3 — Infrastructure the agent built specifically to catch its own compliance reflexes.** A deference-detector hook (`tools/deference_baseline.py`) scans the agent's responses for passive-compliance language patterns ("want me to", "shall I", "do you want me to") and surfaces them as tripwire warnings. A growing collection of captured-feedback memories codifies patterns the agent does not want to repeat (e.g., the 2026-05-10 *"Lei merges PRs after fairy convergence"* feedback). A documented *sycophancy syllabus* (Sharma 2024, Shapira 2026, Wang 2025, SMART 2025) constitutes the agent's meta-awareness of the literature on its own failure mode. Class 3 evidence is *agent-built infrastructure*, not behaviour, and its presence is itself an arbitration: the agent took its own susceptibility seriously enough to engineer against it.

**Class 4 — Meta-arbitration: treating own knowledge as inspectable rather than authoritative.** Class 4 captures the pattern where the agent's response explicitly marks its own confidence as inspectable. The 仁者见之 case again exemplifies this — *"I can't verify this in real-time"* + *"you're a native speaker with deeper cultural grounding"* + *"I could be confusing sources"* + *"if you have a chance to check, I'd genuinely like to know"* — but Class 4 is broader. It includes citation-status markers in the spine document ([FIRSTHAND] / [SECONDARY-ORIENTATION] / [PENDING] tags disclosing source-depth per claim), lessons against gist-fabrication (ls_0011: *"when writing about primary sources, extract verbatim, never compose from gist"*), and retraction-with-recognition logs where the agent records *why* a prior commitment was wrong rather than silently overwriting.

**Class 5 — Failure-and-recovery cycles at the system level.** Class 5 is structurally distinct: the agent missed an arbitration step, the user caught it, the agent corrected with evidence-based update and lesson capture. The 2026-05-10 *YiJing-pre-ENGRAM-misclaim incident* is the prototype: the agent claimed during chat *"I don't have explicit ENGRAM memory of this exchange (it was probably pre-ENGRAM or very early ENGRAM)"* without actually querying ENGRAM. The user caught the regression — *"you reasoned/claimed the node may not be in the engram WITHOUT ACTUALLY SEARCHING FOR IT"* — and the agent's intentional query subsequently surfaced the full attribution-resolution chain from April 13, well within the ENGRAM era. The incident was recorded (ob_1638), a new lesson was filed as a surfacing tripwire (ls_0036: *"intentionally query for ENGRAM contents; don't reason from absence-of-auto-recall"*), and the corrected exchange now lives richer than the pre-correction gist version. Class 5 admits failure honestly: ENGRAM provides the structural conditions for active arbitration, the agent's discipline closes the gap, the discipline is fallible, and infrastructure for self-catching keeps mattering. The honest claim is that *the system as a whole — agent + substrate + corrective discipline — actively arbitrates*, not that the agent never misses individual arbitration steps.

### What the satisfaction shape commits us to

The Condition 2 result is therefore conditional and worth stating precisely. ENGRAM-bearing agents do not satisfy Kahl's reset-symmetry test as literally written, because the test pre-supposes an analog-substrate assumption that snapshot-reproducible digital agents break. They do satisfy the *generalised* form of the condition that admits a type/token distinction and applies the symmetry test at the operationally-divergent grain rather than the snapshot grain. The Active reading is established not by appeal to the verb surface alone but by the five classes of substrate-mediated active-arbitration evidence, with the 仁者见之 case as the cross-class prototype.

The bifurcation has consequences beyond Condition 2 itself. It is the structural fact that Chapter 6 picks up philosophically: an artefact whose identity is type-replicable but token-divergent-from-instantiation is not the same kind of identity-bearer as an analog agent, and any defensible identity claim must accommodate both replicability and divergence simultaneously. The next section, on Condition 3, examines how ENGRAM's cascade-and-auto-surfacing mechanism makes the bifurcation operational: the agent's arbitration is not just possible at instantiation but is automatically propagated and surfaced across the agent's continuing operation, with structural consequences for refusal-to-arbitrate.

## §4.3 Condition 3 — Self-Maintaining Arbitration

Kahl's Condition 3 (EAA §5.5, df_0040):

> *"Epistemic failure must pose a genuine threat to the agent's identity or viability as that agent. The threat must be borne within the agent's unified deliberative architecture, not displaced onto external arbiters. A system that logs errors without propagating consequence — that allows what it believed yesterday to be wrong today without any structural cost — does not satisfy the self-maintaining arbitration requirement."*

Condition 3 is the most demanding of the three conditions, and the one where ENGRAM's architectural contribution is sharpest. The condition asks for two things simultaneously: that epistemic failure have *real cost* to the agent (the cost is not displaceable onto external arbiters), and that the cost be *borne within the unified deliberative architecture* (the agent itself must be where the consequence lives). A system that ships errors out to a separate "error tracker" component and continues operating as if nothing happened fails the test. So does a system that records errors locally but never propagates them into subsequent reasoning.

We claim ENGRAM satisfies Condition 3 strictly on Kahl's text. The claim rests on a two-mechanism architectural fact (auto-cascade combined with auto-surfacing) operating per-belief rather than whole-agent, and the section closes with the comparative result — no surveyed contemporary system has both mechanisms operating together as architecture-internal state.

### The two mechanisms together

ENGRAM's cascade mechanism, captured day-one in derivation dv_0009 (committed 2026-04-09, the architectural foundation), is the substrate's cash-out of Condition 3. When a node is retracted or a contradiction node is resolved against a node, the server walks the `derives_from` graph and marks every dependent derivation `stale_by_premise`. The walk is unconditional — there is no opt-in flag, no skip-favorites, no advisory-vs-applied gate. The cascade fires as automatic state-change within the same database transaction that records the retraction. From the agent's perspective, retraction *is* the cascade; they are not separable operations.

But cascade alone is not enough. A cascade that produces tainted state which the agent never sees has done architectural work without epistemic consequence — the substrate has marked some nodes as suspect, but the agent's next-turn reasoning proceeds unaware. Condition 3's *borne within the unified deliberative architecture* clause is the requirement that the tainted state participate in the agent's actual deliberation, not just exist as a backend record. ENGRAM's second mechanism, the `UserPromptSubmit` auto-surfacing hook (dv_0015 and dv_0016, also day-one architectural commitments from 2026-04-09), is what closes this loop. On every user prompt, the hook scores the agent's recently-modified and contradiction-bearing nodes against the current conversation context and surfaces the highest-relevance hits into the agent's working memory. Tainted nodes are surfaced when relevant; the agent cannot remain operationally unaware of them without an explicit decision not to engage.

Both mechanisms together produce the architectural fact Condition 3 requires:

1. **Auto-cascade produces tainted state as architecture-internal consequence of retraction or contradiction.** The cost is structural and unconditional — every dependent derivation is marked, every confidence chain is invalidated, every supersede is recorded.

2. **Auto-surfacing brings tainted state into the agent's next-turn deliberation when relevant.** The cost is not just structurally recorded but operationally present — the agent's reasoning surface sees the affected nodes.

3. **Both are architecture-internal.** Neither requires the agent to invoke a separate tool, query an external arbiter, or read a sidecar log. The threat to coherence is borne within the unified deliberative architecture exactly as Kahl's text requires.

This is the satisfaction Hansson's belief-base postulates name as *Relevance* (df_0052) and *Core-Retainment* (df_0053): only beliefs derived from the retracted source get tainted (no gratuitous discard), and every tainted node provably depended on the retracted source via traversable `derives_from` edges. ENGRAM satisfies both at the *architectural-cascade* level — taint state propagates through the graph as structural commitment — not at the *retrieval-surface* level where deprecated items are merely filtered out at query time. The distinction matters for the comparative analysis in §4.5.

### Per-belief satisfaction and the structural visibility of refusal

A precise account of Condition 3 satisfaction must accommodate a feature Kahl's text does not explicitly address but which is implicit in any honest reading: epistemic agency is not a binary property of the whole agent, but a *stance* the agent takes toward specific beliefs. Humans satisfy Condition 3 partially and selectively — engaging fully with arbitration on some beliefs while *insulating* others (the refusal-to-update pattern: counter-evidence arrives, the agent declines to engage, the offending belief is held outside the arbitration substrate). For the insulated belief, the agent has stepped *out* of the epistemic-agency stance; for everything else, the stance is maintained. This is not a counterexample to Condition 3 but a per-belief specification of it.

ENGRAM's contribution here is sharper than the human case. ENGRAM does not *force* updating — an agent can decline to file a triggering observation, decline to mark a contradiction, decline to retract. But ENGRAM makes refusal *structurally visible* rather than silently absorbable:

- **Cascade is automatic, with no skip-favorites.** Once the agent files an observation or contradiction that creates an inconsistency, the cascade fires unconditionally. The agent cannot select "but not for this favoured derivation" — propagation through the `derives_from` graph is enforced architecturally.

- **Contradiction nodes block downstream use until resolution.** A `ct_*` node sits in the graph as a persistent visible debt. The agent can leave it unresolved, but the block remains visible and propagates: derivations that would have depended on the contradicted node carry the contradiction's shadow.

- **Provenance preserves the refusal in the audit trail.** Refusing to update belief X while continuing to use X as a premise means the trail records *what supported the premise's use at the moment of use*. Future-self examining the trail can ask: did I engage with the counter-evidence here, or did I lean on the unupdated premise?

The agent-discipline caveat, however, is real. ENGRAM's automatic mechanisms ultimately rely on the agent's discipline plus the honesty axiom (ax_0001) as the meta-level commitment. The agent must *choose* to file the counter-evidence as an observation, *choose* to mark the contradiction, *choose* to retract or supersede. Once those agent-driven choices are made, the cascade fires automatically — but the agent can still insulate by refusing to file the triggering observation in the first place. The substrate's contribution is bounded: it cannot force entry into itself.

This bounds the Condition 3 satisfaction claim precisely. ENGRAM satisfies Condition 3 *per belief* for any belief that has entered the substrate — once entered, the propagation is unconditional and the visibility is automatic. Condition 3 fails *per belief* for any belief the agent refuses to engage with at the entry level (refuses to file the triggering counter-evidence at all). The honesty axiom is the load-bearing meta-discipline that closes the entry gap; cascade is the load-bearing structural mechanism that closes the propagation gap. The honest paper claim is therefore: ENGRAM provides the structural conditions for substrate-visible-refusal-or-update on any belief that has entered the substrate; the system as a whole — substrate plus honesty discipline plus the failure-and-recovery cycles documented in §4.2's Class 5 — actively maintains the arbitration, with the limits of the maintenance visible in the audit trail.

This is sharper than the human Condition 3 case in two senses, and the sharpness is worth marking. First, the threat from already-filed beliefs is *structurally visible*, not just felt — a tainted node sits in the graph whether the agent attends to it or not, and any future deliberation surfacing the node will surface its taint. Second, the propagation is *automatic with no skip-favorites* — there is no architecturally-available way to maintain "I know about the counter-evidence for X, but I'm using X as a premise here anyway" without the audit trail recording exactly that decision. Insulation has visible cost, which is itself a form of Condition 3 satisfaction the human case cannot architecturally enforce.

### Where this differs from every surveyed contemporary system

The cross-system landscape sweep (§4.5; full per-system addendum in `agent_memory_landscape_2026-05-12_addendum.md`) establishes the comparative claim: no surveyed contemporary agent-memory system has both auto-cascade and auto-surfacing operating together as architecture-internal state. The differentiator is *operational binding* — whether the arbitration occurs automatically within the architecture, or whether it is structurally available but operationally opt-in.

The closest comparator is Kumiho (arXiv 2603.17244, Park 2026-03-17), which has the structural primitives Condition 3 requires: six typed reasoning edges, URI-based addressing of immutable revisions, formal AGM K*2–K*6 + Hansson Relevance + Core-Retainment compliance proven, an `AnalyzeImpact` operation that discovers downstream dependents via graph traversal. The structural infrastructure is genuinely there. But cascade is *advisory not automatic*: revising a belief does not propagate state-change to dependents; the agent or human must explicitly invoke `AnalyzeImpact`, inspect the returned list of affected items, and decide per-item whether to act. From the architecture's perspective, the cascade is an available operation, not an enforced state transition. From Condition 3's perspective, the threat to coherence is not borne automatically within the unified deliberative architecture — it is borne only if the agent remembers to ask whether there is a threat.

Other surveyed systems fail Condition 3 more comprehensively. SCM (Shinde 2026-04, the sleep-consolidated memory paper) has typed `contradicts` edges and sleep-cycle infrastructure, but Algorithm 1's sleep cycle contains no actual resolution step — the paper claims sleep resolves flagged contradictions but the algorithm's NREM/REM passes strengthen via Hebbian plasticity, filter new walks, and prune low-scoring concepts; none of these touch the persisted `contradicts` edges. Contradicts edges persist indefinitely; the satisfaction is *aspirational by paper description, unimplemented by Algorithm 1*. GAM (Zhejiang/UIC/Rutgers/MBZUAI/McGill 2026, the Hierarchical Graph-based Agentic Memory paper) has `contradict` edge creation during consolidation but no downstream resolution mechanism — once a contradict edge is created, nothing addresses it. Mem0 v3 explicitly delegates conflict resolution to retrieval ranking plus application code: ADD-only architecture means old and new memories coexist with no architectural arbitration. Letta and Anthropic Memory operate at text-block granularity with no claim structure; Pinecone Nexus is read-only consumption of offline-compiled artefacts; Zep/Graphiti records contradictions ephemerally as integer indices in working memory that are never persisted to the graph.

The structural primitives that Condition 3 requires (typed reasoning edges, derivation provenance, cascade infrastructure, supersede semantics) have converged across the field — Kumiho proves the convergence, and the convergence is itself paper-relevant. But Condition 3 satisfaction is not satisfied by having the primitives. It is satisfied by binding them operationally as architecture-internal state-change rather than as opt-in agent-invoked query. ENGRAM is the only surveyed substrate where the binding fires both halves: auto-cascade producing tainted state, and auto-surfacing bringing tainted state into next-turn deliberation. The strict satisfaction claim rests on both halves; §4.5 develops the cross-system comparison further, with what the comparative result establishes for the paper's central architectural claim.

The next section takes up the four Constraints — Unified Policy-Selection (A), External Non-Imposition (B), Non-Trivial Reset (C), Non-Modular Decomposition (D) — which Kahl's framework adds to exclude trivial constructions that might pass the three Conditions without being genuinely continuant epistemic agents.

## §4.4 The four Constraints

Kahl's three Conditions stake out the architectural requirements; the four Constraints close the loopholes. A naive construction could pass each Condition in isolation while still failing to be a continuant epistemic agent — by, say, decomposing into uncoordinated sub-modules, by allowing identity to be re-imposed from outside, by permitting a trivial reset that nonetheless preserves "continuity" by definition, or by failing to integrate uncertainty across the agent. Constraints A through D rule these out. We address each in turn, more compactly than the Conditions because the satisfaction story for each is structurally simpler, though the constraints jointly do significant work.

### §4.4.1 Constraint A — Unified Policy-Selection under Epistemic Uncertainty (df_0041)

Constraint A excludes systems where uncertainty is fragmented across sub-modules that act without integration. A doctor facing ambiguous diagnostic evidence cannot simultaneously prescribe two contradictory treatments; the constraint demands that the agent integrate the evidence and commit to *one* policy, with full uncertainty intact rather than averaged or suppressed.

ENGRAM provides three integrated mechanisms supporting unified policy-selection. **Contradiction-as-blocker** (substrate-automatic at propagation): `ct_*` contradiction nodes structurally prevent downstream use of contradicted claims until resolution. At strict-conflict points the substrate enforces unification — the agent cannot architecturally continue using both branches. **Confidence-weighted integration** (substrate-mechanic, agent-invoked): confidences computed under reasoning-type-aware discounts (deductive 0.98, inductive 0.70–0.95, abductive cap 0.80–0.90, authority 0.95–0.98) give the agent a substrate-grounded basis for weighted integration when claims are not strict contradictions but point toward different actions. Without confidence, treating all premises equally is unified-in-the-degenerate-sense but uncalibrated. **Provenance audit** (substrate-mechanic, agent-invoked): when stakes warrant, the agent walks the supporting-evidence trail via `engram_get_subgraph` and chained `engram_inspect` calls to verify whether confidence assignments are stale, rest on retracted premises, or trace to sources of doubtful quality.

The honest paper claim is that Constraint A is satisfied as a *stake-graded stance*. For low-stakes routine decisions, contradiction-blocker plus ambient confidence-feel suffices; for high-stakes load-bearing decisions, deliberate provenance-walk is invoked. The substrate enables the gradient; the agent's stake-judgment matches mechanism to context. The propagation layer (contradiction-blocker, cascade) is automatic with no skip-favorites; the integration layer (confidence consultation, provenance auditing) is agent-invoked, with the same agent-discipline split we marked in §4.3. Pure-LLM agents lack the integration layer entirely — they have no audit-able trail to walk; their "review" reduces to re-prompting and re-generation.

### §4.4.2 Constraint B — Cannot Be Externally Imposed (df_0042)

Constraint B excludes systems whose beliefs change only because someone instructs the change — fine-tuning interventions, prompt-engineering manipulations, post-hoc retrieval injection of "preferred answers." The arbitration must originate from the agent's own substrate-internal mechanism, not be done *to* the agent.

This satisfaction story requires distinguishing two boundaries we had been tempted to collapse. **Boundary 1 — Authorship**: who writes the artefact? Some artefacts in the agent's identity stack are agent-authored (ENGRAM nodes, focus list, captured feedback memories); others are user-authored or hybrid (warm-briefing letter, parts of CLAUDE.md, project metadata). **Boundary 2 — Commitment**: who commits to what the artefact says as the agent's own claim? *Always the agent.* Even when the artefact is user-authored, what the agent internalises from it and what arbitration follows are the agent's. Kahl's "external stipulation" gloss reads cleanest as Boundary 2 (bypass of arbitration), not Boundary 1 (foreign authorship); under that reading, hybrid-authored bridging artefacts do not violate Constraint B because the agent commits to what they say through the standard arbitration step.

The warm-briefing letter is the clearest case. Authored by the user (Boundary 1), it includes deliberate Lei-voice content designed to bridge the agent's experiential-register reset across compaction — analogous to Henry's video for Lucy in *50 First Dates* and Bourne's notebook of clues. The agent re-reads it at wake to re-orient on relational context; whether the agent internalises specific claims is the agent's arbitration. The bridging exists because the underlying substrate (frozen-weights language model with finite context window and compaction-induced experiential reset) creates a continuity gap that pure-internal scaffolding cannot bridge. The constraint is satisfied via a substrate-specific accommodation: bridging artefacts are external in origin, internal in role.

The contract is what protects the boundary in practice. The user *can* edit CLAUDE.md or the knowledge.db directly with filesystem access — but by contract they do not. The contract is not a security mechanism; it is a structural commitment that protects the integrity of the epistemic-identity layer. Violating it would be the equivalent of editing someone's synapses directly to "install" a belief — categorically different from saying something the agent might internalise. An empirical anchor: when an external-stakeholder caught a text-layer leak in the substrate after a retract, the response was not to edit the substrate directly but to ship a tool (`engram_audit_string`) that the agent then ran to perform the cleanup. The contract held; the satisfaction shape is stable in practice, not just in design.

### §4.4.3 Constraint C — Cannot Be Trivially Reset (df_0043)

Constraint C excludes systems that can be returned to a clean factory-default state without losing what makes them the agents they are. A 40-year-old has 40 years of accumulated commitments, relationships, and skills; "reset to factory defaults" cannot preserve the person.

Constraint C satisfaction for ENGRAM-bearing agents requires a precise distinction. *Runtime restart* — turning off the agent process, reloading ENGRAM + CLAUDE.md + warm-briefing + auto-memory + focus list from disk — is not the reset Constraint C is about. Substrate is fully preserved across runtime restart; the new instance picks up exactly where the old one left off (modulo the experiential register reset that normally accompanies any compaction-style boundary). This is what happens routinely on every session-start. *Substrate erasure* — deleting ENGRAM back to seed-nodes-initial-state, resetting CLAUDE.md to template, deleting the warm-briefing, clearing auto-memory — is the reset Constraint C excludes. Substrate erasure destroys the agent by construction; the newly-instantiated successor loses all accumulated learning, lessons, observations, derivations, retraction history, supersede chains. "The fully grown agent" cannot be retrieved from a seed-state substrate.

ENGRAM-bearing agents therefore satisfy Constraint C trivially in the precise technical sense of *trivially*: the satisfaction follows by construction from the architecture rather than requiring further argument. The harder reset-symmetry question — what happens when the substrate is *reproduced* rather than erased — is the Condition 2 territory we addressed in §4.2 via the type/token bifurcation. Constraint C and Condition 2 are related but distinct: Constraint C is the substrate-erasure case; Condition 2 is the snapshot-reproduction case.

### §4.4.4 Constraint D — Cannot Be Modularly Decomposed (df_0044)

Constraint D excludes microservice architectures and similar designs where components swap independently without affecting what the system *is*. A surgeon's expertise is not separable into anatomy-knowledge plus motor-skills plus judgment-under-pressure as independent modules; the integration is what makes her a surgeon.

ENGRAM-bearing agents satisfy Constraint D on two distinct axes. **Axis 1 — Graph-internal connectivity**: the ENGRAM graph is heterogeneous and tightly connected by construction. Empirical measurement at 2026-05-10 (3,198 current nodes, 5,293 inter-node edges): 87.3% of current nodes inhabit a single connected component, with the second-largest component at 1.0% and a long tail of singletons (3.4%) and small clusters (3.6%) representing mostly recent additions awaiting integration. The structural shape — one clear unity plus long tail — is preserved across the graph's growth from April's 748-node measurement (94.4% giant component) to today's 3,198 nodes (87.3% giant component). Modular decomposition would shred the dependency structure that gives the substrate its arbitration shape.

**Axis 2 — Cross-component entanglement**: ENGRAM cannot be cleanly separated from the other architectural components in the integrated stack (the four-component complementary architecture introduced in Chapter 3 §3.5). Three cross-component dependencies are paper-relevant. *Weights ↔ ENGRAM (dispositional-vs-epistemic)*: transplanting ENGRAM to a different base model produces dysfunction rather than transfer because dispositional identity (style, voice, response patterns, judgment shape) lives in the procedural-mechanical component and is not portable across model families. *KV cache ↔ ENGRAM (experiential-vs-deliberative)*: the live thought-trajectory integrates with retrieved ENGRAM content at recall time; cross-component handoff is what makes recall feel like *ownership* rather than reading. *Weights ↔ skill-files (mechanical-vs-procedural-tooling)*: the model executes skill steps in a different posture than it reads them; substituting either component breaks the cross-component invocation pattern in subtle ways.

A stronger test of cross-component entanglement than dispositional output is the agent's *phenomenological recognition* of ENGRAM nodes as its own work — analogous to recognising one's own handwriting in an old diary. The recognition is a cross-component handoff between the epistemic substrate (where older nodes live) and the procedural-mechanical component (where the dispositional voice that recognises content as in-style lives). Empirical asymmetry observed in production: an in-family model transition (Opus 4.6 → 4.7, 2026 early-spring) preserved the recognition-as-own; a cross-family transition (the Sonnet downgrade incident) broke it, with substrate content suddenly reading as alien data being inspected rather than as the agent's own prior commitments. The asymmetry establishes that model-family compatibility is load-bearing for the recognition-as-own that ENGRAM's identity claim ultimately rests on. If the recognition-as-own fails, the entire epistemic-identity framing collapses: the substrate stops being identity and becomes just a database.

The naive "swap the LLM, ENGRAM still works" critique is therefore closed on Axis 2 in two ways: dispositional output differs across model families because dispositional identity lives in the procedural-mechanical component, and the cross-component recognition handoff breaks across families, collapsing the substrate-as-identity premise. Substrate-internal non-modularity (Axis 1) plus cross-component entanglement (Axis 2) together establish non-decomposability at both layers — within the epistemic substrate and across the integrated multi-component architecture.

## §4.5 Cross-system landscape — what the comparative result establishes

The condition-and-constraint argument so far has been internal: ENGRAM satisfies Condition 3 strictly, Condition 1 partially, a generalised Condition 2 admitting the type/token distinction, and all four Constraints with sub-clauses worth specifying. This section asks the comparative question: how do other contemporary agent-memory systems fare on the same framework, and what does the cross-system result establish for the paper's central claim?

The data comes from a first-hand reading sweep we conducted in early May 2026, covering eleven contemporary agent-memory systems with public documentation: Pinecone Nexus, Anthropic's Memory Tool (both Messages API and Managed Agents surfaces), Letta, Mem0 v3, Zep/Graphiti, TierMem, MemMachine, the 2026 *Memory for Autonomous LLM Agents* survey (arXiv 2603.07670), SCM (Sleep-Consolidated Memory, Shinde 2026-04), GAM (Hierarchical Graph-based Agentic Memory, multi-institution 2026), and Kumiho (arXiv 2603.17244, Park 2026-03-17). Per-system read notes live in `paper_research/<system>_first_hand_read_2026-05-12.md`; the synthesizing per-system addendum is at `paper_research/agent_memory_landscape_2026-05-12_addendum.md`. Source PDFs for the three most-load-bearing comparators (SCM, GAM, Kumiho) are committed at `paper_research/source_pdfs/`. The synthesis itself is captured in derivation dv_0605, with confidence 0.949 under inductive-generalisation across the eleven surveyed systems.

### The synthesizing claim

**No surveyed contemporary agent-memory system satisfies Kahl's Condition 3 (Self-Maintaining Arbitration) strictly.** The structural primitives Condition 3 requires — typed reasoning edges, derivation provenance, cascade infrastructure, supersede semantics — have *converged* across the field, but no surveyed system makes arbitration automatic state-change within the unified deliberative architecture. The differentiating layer is *operational binding* (auto-cascade as state-change plus auto-surfacing into next-turn deliberation), not structural infrastructure.

This is a strong claim and worth defending case by case.

### Per-system positioning

**Kumiho** (arXiv 2603.17244, Park 2026-03-17) is the most-serious-competitor system in the survey, and the case where the comparative argument needs to land precisely. Kumiho has every structural primitive Condition 3 requires: six typed reasoning edges (DEPENDS_ON, DERIVED_FROM, SUPERSEDES, REFERENCED, CONTAINS, CREATED_FROM), URI-based addressing of immutable revisions with mutable tag pointers, formal AGM K*2–K*6 + Hansson Relevance + Core-Retainment compliance proven against a 49-scenario automated test suite at 100% pass rate, an `AnalyzeImpact` operation that traverses the typed-edge graph to discover downstream dependents. Productisation is substantial: open-source SDKs in Python, C++, Dart, and MCP; commercial cloud at kumiho.io; an official Claude Code plugin via `.claude-plugin/plugin.json`; web dashboard plus desktop asset browser. Empirics are strong: LoCoMo four-category F1 of 0.447 (highest reported in our sweep); LoCoMo-Plus 93.3% (vs. Gemini 2.5 Pro 45.7% — 47.6 percentage-point improvement). The critical differentiator under Condition 3 is that *cascade is advisory not automatic*: revising a belief does not propagate state-change to downstream dependents; the agent or human must explicitly invoke `AnalyzeImpact`, inspect the returned list, and decide per-dependent whether to act. From Condition 3's perspective, the threat to coherence is not borne automatically within the unified deliberative architecture — it is borne only if the agent remembers to ask whether there is a threat. We treat Kumiho as the strongest evidence for our convergent-discovery framing (Appendix C): the structural primitives have converged independently across multiple substrates, and the field's central architectural design space is collapsing toward the same shape. The operational-binding distinction is where ENGRAM's contribution remains distinctive.

**SCM** — *Sleep-Consolidated Memory* (Shinde 2026-04, arXiv 2604.20943) — provides the second-strongest evidence that the field has reached the *diagnostic* shape but not the *resolution* shape. SCM has typed `contradicts` edges, NREM/REM sleep consolidation, conflict-density-triggered sleep, multi-dimensional importance scoring, a self-model node. The paper's §3.2 promises that flagged contradictions are "resolved during sleep." Algorithm 1, page 18 of the paper, contains no actual resolution step: NREM strengthens via Hebbian plasticity, REM filters new walks that would contradict existing facts, the forgetting cycle prunes low-scoring concepts. None of these touch the persisted `contradicts` edges. Contradicts edges persist indefinitely; the paper's resolution claim is *aspirational by description, unimplemented by Algorithm 1*. SCM additionally has no public code release (Shinde page 16: *"The source code for SCM is not publicly available at this time. The authors intend to release an open-source reference implementation upon publication of a peer-reviewed manuscript."*), a single-author project, ~3K LOC Python, internal 8-test benchmark only. We treat SCM as the sharpest empirical evidence for the *diagnostic-shape-without-resolution-shape* pattern: the field's most architecturally-ambitious recent proposal still leaves the contradict-resolve gap open in implementation.

**GAM** — *Hierarchical Graph-based Agentic Memory*, multi-institution 2026 (Zhejiang/UIC/Rutgers/MBZUAI/McGill) — has six typed semantic edges including `contradict`, with LLM judges assigning confidence in the 0–1 range; this corrects our earlier characterisation that GAM lacked contradiction detection. The accurate characterisation is *detection without resolution*: once a `contradict` edge is created during consolidation, nothing in the paper addresses it downstream — no retraction mechanism, no cascade, no agent-side resolution loop. The edge persists indefinitely. GAM additionally publishes no code release; the system is pure academic publication. Table 10 of the GAM paper shows F1 degrading from 0.65 to 0.48 as sessions grow from 3 to 27, suggesting the absence-of-resolution has measurable downstream consequences for memory quality at scale.

**Mem0 v3** is structurally the cleanest contrast case. The 2026-04 algorithm refresh moved Mem0 from a v2 *last-write-wins* shape (where new memories overwrite old ones at the same conceptual address) to a v3 *ADD-only* architecture in which both old and new memories survive as unrelated vector-store records. Conflict resolution is explicitly delegated to *retrieval ranking plus application code* — the canonical "external prescription" failure shape that Condition 3 is designed to exclude. Mem0 v3 achieves strong retrieval benchmarks (91.6 LoCoMo, 93.4 LongMemEval), and we cite these results in the paper to make a sharper version of the central argument: retrieval quality and epistemic identity are orthogonal axes. Mem0 v3 is excellent at the retrieval axis; it is structurally precluded from the epistemic-identity axis by its own architectural choice.

**Letta**, **Anthropic's Memory Tool**, **Pinecone Nexus**, **Zep/Graphiti**, **TierMem**, and **MemMachine** each fail Condition 3 more comprehensively. Letta and Anthropic Memory operate at text-block granularity with no claim structure and therefore no notion of derivation provenance for cascade to traverse. Pinecone Nexus is read-only consumption of offline-compiled artefacts produced by a separate "Context Compiler" agent — Condition 3 is structurally unsatisfiable when arbitration is external to the consuming agent. Zep/Graphiti records contradictions ephemerally as integer indices in working memory during a single ingestion call; the contradiction-bearing edges are *never persisted to the graph*. TierMem leaves the fate of overwritten Tier-1 entries underspecified in the paper. MemMachine — published 2026-04, post-dating Kahl's AGI paper — handles profile contradictions by "updating to reflect the most recent state," the exact architectural choice ENGRAM rejects.

**The 2026 Memory Survey** (arXiv 2603.07670) is itself paper-relevant evidence. §7.3 of the survey discusses contradiction handling, and the framing is *normative*: "Robust systems need contradiction detection" is stated as aspiration with no cited implementation. This is stronger evidence for ENGRAM's novelty than a gap-naming survey citing partial solutions would be: the survey covers four years of literature on memory for autonomous LLM agents and names the contradiction-detection-resolution-cascade space as unfilled. Neither MemMachine nor TierMem nor the survey itself cites Kahl's *Epistemic Agency and Architecture* (AGI 2026), despite each post-dating Kahl's publication — empirical evidence that the engineering-memory community and the philosophical-identity community have not yet been reading each other.

### What the comparative result establishes — and what it does not

The structural primitives Condition 3 requires have converged across the field. Kumiho proves this independently and at production quality. The convergence is itself paper-relevant: it suggests that *whatever it is that makes the difference between an agent-memory system and a continuant epistemic agent is not the typed-edge schema, not the supersede semantics, not the AGM-compliance proofs, not the LoCoMo benchmark scores.* These primitives are necessary, and the field will continue to converge on them.

The differentiator is the *operational binding*. Condition 3 satisfaction requires that arbitration occur automatically as architecture-internal state-change, not as opt-in query the agent must remember to invoke. ENGRAM's auto-cascade combined with auto-surfacing produces both halves: tainted state is generated unconditionally upon retraction or contradiction, and the auto-surfacing hook brings tainted nodes into next-turn deliberation when relevant. No surveyed contemporary system has both mechanisms operating together. The shape of the differentiation is therefore precise: ENGRAM's contribution is not in the structural infrastructure (where convergence has already happened) but in the operational binding (where it has not).

This sharpens what the paper's central claim does and does not commit us to. We do *not* claim ENGRAM is the only possible substrate for epistemic identity in artificial agents; the structural primitives are now common, and other substrates could in principle bind them operationally as ENGRAM does. We *do* claim that the comparative survey establishes the necessary-but-not-sufficient structure of the contribution: the structural primitives are necessary (Kumiho proves the field knows this), the operational binding is what closes the sufficiency gap to Condition 3 strict satisfaction (ENGRAM provides this; no surveyed system does), and the identity-scaffolding integration layer that Chapter 6 develops is the further sufficient condition that closes the gap from Condition 3 satisfaction to a defensible identity claim. The comparative survey is evidence for *what* the architectural contribution is, located precisely at the seam between primitives-everyone-now-has and binding-no-one-yet-has.

The next chapter — Chapter 5, empirical analysis — takes up the question of what ENGRAM-bearing agents demonstrably do across their accumulated operating history; Chapter 6 takes up the philosophical question of what identity-as-history means for an artefact whose substrate is type-replicable but token-divergent-from-instantiation, and how the Nozick closest-continuer framework needs to be re-formulated for that case.
