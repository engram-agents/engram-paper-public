## Locke — *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689/1694), Book II, Ch. xxvii "Of Identity and Diversity"
Source: Project Gutenberg eBook 10615 (Vol. I), clean text. Backs Appendix **B.1**.

**§9 — consciousness reaching back (the core claim):**
> "as far as this consciousness can be extended backwards to any past action or thought, so far reaches the identity of that person; it is the same self now it was then; and it is by the same self with this present one that now reflects on it, that that action was done."

**§15 — the prince and the cobbler:**
> "For should the soul of a prince, carrying with it the consciousness of the prince's past life, enter and inform the body of a cobbler, as soon as deserted by his own soul, every one sees he would be the same PERSON with the prince, accountable only for the prince's actions..."

**§26 (Gutenberg heads it "28. Person a forensic Term") — the forensic self:**
> "PERSON, as I take it, is the name for this self. ... It is a forensic term, appropriating actions and their merit; and so belongs only to intelligent agents, capable of a law, and happiness, and misery."

✓ Backs B.1 ("consciousness can be extended backwards"; prince & cobbler; forensic / "appropriates"). All three verbatim.

## Reid — *Essays on the Intellectual Powers of Man* (1785), Essay III, Ch. 6 "Of Mr Locke's Account of our Personal Identity"
Source: scholarly course PDF (thatmarcusfamily.org, original text — minor scan artifacts "ofhis", "~hem" in non-load-bearing spots). Backs Appendix **B.2** (transitivity / brave-officer).

**The brave-officer case + the contradiction it forces:**
> "Suppose a brave officer to have been flogged when a boy at school for robbing an orchard, to have taken a standard from the enemy in his first campaign, and to have been made a general in advanced life; suppose, also, ... that, when he took the standard, he was conscious of his having been flogged at school, and that, when made a general, he was conscious of his taking the standard, but had absolutely lost the consciousness of his flogging."

> "Therefore the general is, and at the same time is not, the same person with him who was flogged at school."

✓ Backs B.2 (Locke's recall-criterion breaks transitivity). Verbatim (load-bearing sentence clean).

## Butler — "Of Personal Identity" (First Dissertation appended to *The Analogy of Religion*, 1736)
Source: Christian Classics Ethereal Library full text (ccel.org). Backs Appendix **B.2** (circularity).

**The circularity objection (the canonical line):**
> "And one should really think it self-evident, that consciousness of personal identity presupposes, and therefore cannot constitute, personal identity; any more than knowledge, in any other case, can constitute truth, which it presupposes."

## Clark & Chalmers — "The Extended Mind", *Analysis* 58(1), 1998
Source: David Chalmers' own posted text (consc.net/papers/extended.html). Backs Appendix **B.8** (extended mind, Otto, past-endorsement).

**The four features of the Otto case — note the fourth (past conscious endorsement), which B.8 leans on:**
> "First, the notebook is a constant in Otto's life... Second, the information in the notebook is directly available without difficulty. Third, upon retrieving information from the notebook he automatically endorses it. Fourth, the information in the notebook has been consciously endorsed at some point in the past, and indeed is there as a consequence of this endorsement."

And the constitutive hint (footnote) B.8's "second-wave / constitutive" framing rests on:
> "The constancy and past-endorsement criteria may suggest that history is partly constitutive of belief."

✓ Backs B.8 ("past conscious endorsement"; ENGRAM meets it at entry + substrate level). Verbatim. (Note: the *four criteria* are C&C's; the "second-wave EMT" framing in B.8 is later-literature, cited there via ob_1568 — keep that attribution distinct.)

## Damasio — *The Feeling of What Happens* (1999), Ch. 6, Table 6.1 "Kinds of Self" (p. 174); first introduced pp. 17–18
Source: full-book scan, clean OCR text layer (artifact: "something-to-beknown" = "something-to-be-known"). Backs Appendix **B.7** (autobiographical self).

**Table 6.1 — the definition (p. 174):**
> "AUTOBIOGRAPHICAL SELF: The autobiographical self is based on autobiographical memory which is constituted by implicit memories of multiple instances of individual experience of the past and of the anticipated future. The invariant aspects of an individual's biography form the basis for autobiographical memory. ... Sets of memories which describe identity and person can be reactivated as a neural pattern and made explicit as images whenever needed."

**First introduction (pp. 17–18):**
> "My term for that entity is the autobiographical self. The autobiographical self depends on systematized memories of situations in which core consciousness was involved in the knowing of the most invariant characteristics of an organism's life—who you were born to, where, when, your likes and dislikes, the way you usually react to a problem or a conflict, your name, and so on."

✓ Backs B.7 (autobiographical self = temporally-extended identity from implicit past+anticipated-future memories; cleanest ENGRAM map *if embodiment bracketed*). Verbatim (split-word artifact noted).

## Ricoeur — *Oneself as Another* (Soi-même comme un autre, 1990; trans. Kathleen Blamey, Univ. Chicago Press 1992)
Source: full OCR text (epdf.pub), Blamey translation confirmed from the title page. Backs Appendix **B.5** (idem/ipse). Page locators are approximate (embedded running-headers).

**Introduction ("The Question of Selfhood", ~p.2) — the idem/ipse distinction:**
> "The second philosophical intention, implicitly present in the title in the word 'self,' is to distinguish two major meanings of 'identity' ... depending on whether one understands by 'identical' the equivalent of the Latin *ipse* or *idem*."

**Introduction — the ipse thesis (selfhood ≠ unchanging core):**
> "Our thesis throughout will be that identity in the sense of *ipse* implies no assertion concerning some unchanging core of the personality."

**Fifth Study ("Personal Identity and Narrative Identity", ~p.118) — character (idem) vs. keeping-one's-word (ipse):**
> "There is, in fact, another model of permanence in time besides that of character. It is that of keeping one's word in faithfulness to the word that has been given. ... Keeping one's word expresses a self-constancy which cannot be inscribed, as character was, within the dimension of something in general but solely within the dimension of 'who?'"

✓ Backs B.5 (idem = persistence-of-marks/character; ipse = selfhood holding as marks change). Verbatim (OCR artifact "baring"→"bearing" in a non-quoted spot).

## Stiegler — *Technics and Time, 1: The Fault of Epimetheus* (1994; trans. Beardsworth & Collins, Stanford UP 1998)
Source: archive.org djvu OCR text. Backs Appendix **B.8** (externalized memory constitutes, not merely supports).

**HONEST CITATION NUANCE (must shape B.8).** In *Technics and Time 1* the term **"tertiary retention" appears essentially once**; Stiegler mostly develops the concept under **"tertiary memory"** and **"epiphylogenesis."** The explicit "tertiary retention" coinage is more prominent in *later* Stiegler (TT2/TT3). So B.8's headline term "tertiary retention" is defensible from TT1's single use, but the *constitutive* claim lives in the "tertiary memory"/"epiphylogenesis" passages below — cite those for the load-bearing point, and either soften "tertiary retention" to "tertiary/external memory" or footnote the later-Stiegler source for the developed term.

**The single TT1 "tertiary retention" use (glossing Husserl):**
> "...the opposition that Husserl sets up in his analysis of the temporal object between primary, secondary, and tertiary retention (we call tertiary retention what Husserl designates by 'image-consciousness')..."

**Epiphylogenesis defined (~p.140):**
> "this epigenetic sedimentation, a memorization of what has come to pass, is what is called the past, what we shall name the epiphylogenesis of man, meaning the conservation, accumulation, and sedimentation of successive epigeneses, mutually articulated."

**Epiphylogenesis CONSTITUTES identity (~p.140) — the load-bearing quote for B.8:**
> "Epiphylogenesis bestows its identity upon the human individual: the accents of his speech, the style of his approach, the force of his gesture, the unity of his world."

**Constitutivity of tertiary memory (p.255):**
> "This is the meaning of epiphylogenesis. A tool functions first as image-consciousness. This constitutivity of 'tertiary memory' grounds the irreducible neutrality of the who..."

✓ Backs B.8 (externalized/technical memory constitutes identity, not merely supports it). Verbatim (OCR double-spaces/hyphen-breaks normalized in the quotes; concept-term nuance flagged above).

## Tulving — "Memory and consciousness," *Canadian Psychology* 26(1):1–12, 1985
Source: Tulving's own works archive (alicekim.ca), scanned PDF → my OCR pass (`ocr` tool, page-1 mean_conf 0.78). Backs Appendix **B.7** (autonoetic/noetic). OCR artifacts corrected in [brackets]; the definition matches the canonical secondary-quoted form, which cross-validates the OCR.

**The three-way taxonomy (Abstract):**
> "...different memory systems (procedural, semantic, and episodic) and corresponding varieties [of] consciousness (anoetic, noetic, and autonoetic), with special emphasis on episodic memory and autonoetic consciousness as its necessary correlate..."

**Autonoetic consciousness defined (Abstract):**
> "Autonoetic (self-knowing) consciousness is the name given [to] the kind of consciousness that mediates an individual['s] awareness of his [or] her existence and identity in subjective time extending from the personal past through the present [to] the personal future[.] It provides the characteristic phenomenal flavour of the experience of remembering."

✓ Backs B.7 (autonoetic = self-knowing, re-experiencing, tied to episodic memory + identity-in-subjective-time; the phenomenal flavour ENGRAM does NOT claim — the noetic/autonoetic boundary). Verbatim from OCR, artifacts bracketed.

## Peirce — type/token (CP 4.537, "Prolegomena to an Apology for Pragmaticism", *The Monist*, 1906)
Ch2 §2.4 machinery (df_0047). Source: the passage as quoted verbatim in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy "Types and Tokens" entry from CP 4.537 (a reliable host of the primary; primary of-record is *Collected Papers* vol. 4 §537). Public domain.

**Type and token defined (CP 4.537):**
> "...it is impossible that this word should lie visibly on a page or be heard in any voice, for the reason that it is not a Single thing or Single event. It does not exist; it only determines things that do exist. Such a definitely significant Form, I propose to term a Type. A Single event which happens once and whose identity is limited to that one happening ... such as this or that word on a single line of [print], I will venture to call a Token."

**The "twenty 'the's" example:**
> "A common mode of estimating the amount of matter in a MS. or printed book is to count the number of words. There will ordinarily be about twenty the's on a page, and of course they count as twenty words. In another sense of the word 'word,' however, there is but one word 'the' in the English language..."

✓ Backs Ch2 §2.4 (type = "definitely significant Form" that "does not exist; it only determines things that do exist"; token = "Single event which happens once"). Verbatim. (The paper's *digital* type/token application is flagged in the draft as our extension — Peirce does not discuss the computational case.)

## Hansson — Core-Retainment / Relevance (belief-base contraction; Ch2 §2.3, df_0053)
Source: the postulate as stated verbatim in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy "Logic of Belief Revision" entry (a reliable host quoting Hansson's formulation; primary of-record is Hansson, *A Textbook of Belief Dynamics*, 1999, and his base-contraction papers). Confirms df_0053's "verbatim sense" gloss is accurate.

**Core-retainment (belief-set form, SEP/Hansson):**
> "If q ∈ K and q ∉ K÷p, then there is a belief set K' such that K' ⊆ K and that p ∉ K' but p ∈ K'+q. Core-retainment requires of an excluded sentence q that it in some way contributes to the fact that K implies p."

**Belief-base form** (the level ENGRAM lives at — finite bases, not consequence-closed sets; from the base-contraction literature): *If ψ ∈ (B \ (B−φ)), then there is some B' ⊆ B such that B' ⊭ φ but B' ∪ {ψ} ⊨ φ.*

**Load-bearing nuance for Ch2 §2.3.** SEP notes that on belief *sets*, an operator satisfying Core-retainment *also satisfies Recovery* — so on closed sets the two are not cleanly separable. This is precisely why Hansson re-grounds on belief *bases*, where Recovery fails but Core-retainment holds. Ch2 §2.3 already makes this move (Recovery rejected; Core-retainment + Relevance on bases); the nuance sharpens *why* the base level is the right one. **Relevance** is the (slightly stronger) partial-meet sibling — every operation satisfying Relevance satisfies Core-retainment, but not conversely; ENGRAM's cascade aligns with the weaker, base-appropriate Core-retainment (dv_0613).

✓ df_0053's gloss ("every removed belief must have genuinely contributed to deriving the contracted belief") = Hansson's "the excluded sentence contributes to the fact that K implies p." Verbatim confirmed; df_0053 left as-is (accurate).
