## Method

Same instrument as the prior delta; the model under review changed (round-3 DeepSeek → Opus-5.5), the method did not. Every bullet under each per-section `**5. Open questions**` block in the Opus log was enumerated and classified (the Opus reader does not use "Still open"/"New" tags, so every bullet is filed under the category **Open questions**, except the Abstract's block, filed separately as **Abstract-initial** per the spec, since the Abstract has no round-2/round-1 predecessor to answer). The end-of-paper **Top five edits, ranked** (5 numbered items, each item's full text incl. its own sub-bullets, matching the prior delta's per-numbered-item granularity), **Ordering** (2 bold-led paragraphs in the Opus log, each with its own sub-bullets folded into the item), and **What I still do not understand about the system** (this section in the Opus log is organised as 8 bold subheadings each followed by a bullet list, not numbered prose paragraphs as in the round-3 log; each individual `- ` bullet — 22 total — was treated as one item, since that is the finest natural item boundary the Opus log itself draws — flagged as a judgment call in the handoff) were each enumerated the same way.

Each item was reduced to a distinguishing key phrase and grepped (case-insensitive, whole file, not only open-questions sections) against the full text of round 3, round 2, and round 1. Grep evidence is `file:line` of the first match; word-boundary matching was used for plain-word phrases to avoid substring false positives (e.g. "person" inside "first-person"); phrases containing punctuation (§, `, /, %, formulas) used plain substring matching. The key-phrase grep misses rephrasings, so **GENUINELY NEW is an upper bound** and **REPEAT is a lower bound**. Key phrases were first generated by an automated longest-quoted/backticked/bolded-phrase-first, then longest-discriminating-n-gram heuristic; every phrase reducing to reader-scaffolding ("partly answers", "the earlier open question", bare "New"/"Still open" tags, etc.) or flagged by manual spot-review as topically miscategorised (a coincidental hit on unrelated paper content) was manually replaced with a narrower, topic-specific phrase and re-greped by hand — each such row is marked `(manual: ...)` in the table. Given the volume (227 forward items, 280 reverse items), manual QA was targeted at every 2-word-or-shorter auto-picked key phrase plus every phrase containing reader-scaffolding tokens, not at all 507 rows individually — see the handoff's "Ambiguous decisions" for the residual-risk statement this implies.

## Classification legend

- **REPEAT** — the key phrase (or a close paraphrase) is present in round 3.
- **RESOLVED-THEN-REGRESSED** — absent from round 3, present in round 2 or round 1 (raised earlier, not carried into round 3's open-questions list, and resurfaced — or rather, present in the Opus log but not in the immediately-preceding DeepSeek round).
- **GENUINELY NEW** — absent from all three DeepSeek rounds.

When a key phrase was too generic to discriminate (a hit clearly about something else), a narrower phrase was chosen and the row says so via a `(manual: ...)` note.
