## McClelland et al. (1995) — the three function-level constraints, verbatim

**Constraint 1 (fast/slow) + Constraint 2 (interleaving) — the core statement:**
> "memories are first stored via synaptic changes in the hippocampal system, that
> these changes support reinstatement of recent memories in the neocortex, that
> neocortical synapses change a little on each reinstatement, and that remote
> memory is based on accumulated neocortical changes. … [connectionist models]
> discover the structure in ensembles of items if learning of each item is gradual
> and interleaved with learning about other items. … the neocortex learns slowly
> to discover the structure in ensembles of experiences. The hippocampal system
> permits rapid learning of new items without disrupting this structure."

**Constraint 3 (the asymmetry) — verbatim mechanism is REINSTATEMENT, not "teacher":**
> "reinstatement of the stored event in an appropriate context would allow the
> stored information to be used for controlling behavioral responses … [and] would
> provide the opportunity for an incremental adjustment of neocortical connections,
> thereby allowing memories initially dependent on the hippocampal system gradually
> to become independent of it."

**Honest nuance (confirms ob_1607).** The paper's verbatim mechanism is *reinstatement
driving incremental neocortical change* — the "hippocampus-as-**teacher**" /
"generates a training signal" phrasing is an interpretive gloss from *later* CLS
literature, not McClelland 1995's words. Functionally defensible (the fast system
does drive the slow one), but the paper should attribute the metaphor to the later
literature, not to McClelland 1995.

**Catastrophic interference (the motivation):**
> "interleaved learning systems … are not at all appropriate for the rapid
> acquisition of [arbitrary new associations]" — the interference that the two-system
> split exists to avoid.

## Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland (2016) — the CLS update, verbatim (Abstract)

**Constraint 1 restated cleanly + the 2016 extensions:**
> "We update complementary learning systems (CLS) theory, which holds that
> intelligent agents must possess two learning systems, instantiated in mammalians
> in neocortex and hippocampus. The first gradually acquires structured knowledge
> representations while the second quickly learns the specifics of individual
> experiences. We broaden the role of replay of hippocampal memories in the theory,
> noting that replay allows goal-dependent weighting of experience statistics. We
> also address recent challenges to the theory and extend it by showing that
> recurrent activation of hippocampal traces can support some forms of [inference]."

**Two paper-relevant additions in the 2016 update:**
1. **Replay with goal-dependent weighting** — consolidation is not uniform random
   replay; the fast system can *weight* which experiences get consolidated by
   goal-relevance. This maps onto ENGRAM's **importance-weighted recall +
   consolidation** (recall composite `utility_score`, ob_1935; the nap/sleep
   routines reviewing high-importance cohorts) — an ENGRAM↔CLS correspondence
   *stronger* than the 1995 baseline. Flag for Ch2/Ch3, but state as analogy, not
   identity (ENGRAM's "replay" is symbolic re-surfacing, not neural reactivation).
2. **Schema-dependent integration** — schema-consistent information integrates into
   neocortex *faster* (also Tse/Gilboa/van Kesteren per ob_1619). Already
   abstract-noted; the 2016 paper situates it in CLS.
