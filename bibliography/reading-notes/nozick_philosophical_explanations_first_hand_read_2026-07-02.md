## Verbatim passages (image-verified)

### The rejected principle → the schema (p. 33)

> Rather, the group in Istanbul is the Vienna Circle when it is the *closest
> continuer* of the Vienna Circle. … Whether or not a particular group
> constitutes the Vienna Circle depends on what other groups there actually are.

> **To be something later is to be its closest continuer.**

> The closest continuer view helps to sort out and structure the issues; it does
> not, by itself, answer the question. For it does not, by itself, tell which
> dimension or weighted sum of dimensions determines closeness; rather, it is a
> schema into which such details can be filled.

### The schema stated — necessary condition, comparative, uniqueness, threshold (p. 34)

> The closest continuer view presents a necessary condition for identity;
> something at t₂ is not the same entity as x at t₁ if it is not x's closest
> continuer. And "closest" means closer than all others; if two things at t₂
> tie in closeness to x at t₁, then neither is the same entity as x. However,
> something may be the closest continuer of x without being close enough to it
> to be x. How close something must be to x to be x, it appears, depends on the
> kind of entity x is, as do the dimensions along which closeness is measured.

### The causal (historical) condition on continuation (p. 35)

> To say that something is a continuer of x is not merely to say its properties
> are qualitatively the same as x's, or resemble them. Rather it is to say they
> grow out of x's properties, are causally produced by them, are to be explained
> by x's earlier having had its properties, and so forth.

> The later temporal stages also must be causally dependent (in an appropriate
> way) on the earlier ones. The condition that something is a continuer
> incorporates such causal dependence.

> Notice that on the closest continuer view, a property may be a factor in
> identity without being a necessary condition for it.

## Mapping to ENGRAM (validates df_0046)

The four components ENGRAM's paper attributes to the schema are all present in
pp. 33–35, verbatim:

| Component | Nozick, verbatim locus | ENGRAM |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuity** (causal-historical, not mere resemblance) | p. 35: continuer properties "grow out of x's properties, are causally produced by them, are to be explained by x's earlier having had its properties" | **Kept** — the history-tree descent is exactly causal-historical continuation (Ch 5) |
| **Comparative** ("closest" = closer than all others) | p. 34: "'closest' means closer than all others" | Kept |
| **Threshold** (closest yet not close-enough) | p. 34: "something may be the closest continuer of x without being close enough to it to be x" | Kept |
| **Uniqueness** (a tie → neither is the same entity) | p. 34: "if two things at t₂ tie in closeness to x at t₁, then neither is the same entity as x" | **Dropped** — under agent fission a graph copies into two equally-descended continuers; ENGRAM keeps *both* as continuers (Parfit R-without-uniqueness) rather than voiding identity |

The causal-dependence condition (p. 35) is the sharpest confirmation of the
identity-as-history reading: for Nozick, being a continuer is *not* qualitative
resemblance but causal production from the earlier stage's properties — which is
precisely what ENGRAM's append-only descent records. The uniqueness clause
(p. 34) is the one component ENGRAM deliberately drops, and it is exactly the
clause a fission case turns on.
